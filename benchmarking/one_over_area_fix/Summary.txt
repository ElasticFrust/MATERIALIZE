================================================================================
  Summary: 1/Area Normalization Fix — Investigation & Recommendation
================================================================================

Date:  2026-02-19

Overview
--------
We investigated replacing the hardcoded 1/16 normalization constant in the
D2C (Discrete-to-Continuum) elastic tensor computation with a per-triangle
1/Area_s factor.  The idea was that for non-equilateral triangles the factor
of 1/16 (which equals the area of a unit-side equilateral triangle,
sqrt(3)/4 ≈ 0.433, squared and scaled) is incorrect, and using each
triangle's actual area would properly convert the spring energy into an
energy density.


What Was Changed (3 places)
---------------------------
1. BARE ELASTIC TENSOR:  factor = k / l^2 / (1/16)  -->  k / l^2 / Area_s
   Each triangle's tensor now represents energy per unit area rather than a
   raw spring energy.

2. SELF-CONSISTENT COUPLING (Woodbury solve):
   Uniform 1/N weights  -->  area weights  w_s = Area_s / sum(Area)
   The B-matrix coupling in the self-consistent equation now weights each
   triangle by its fractional area.

3. FINAL AVERAGING:
   Simple mean of per-triangle tensors  -->  area-weighted average.


Empirical Results (20 eta values, 10 trials each, crystal topology)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- At eta = 0 (perfect lattice): old and new D2C both give nu = 1/3.
  No difference, as expected — all triangles are identical equilaterals.

- At eta < 0.25 (moderate perturbation): both track the simulation well,
  with the area-fixed version slightly closer.

- At eta = 0.30–0.45: the old 1/16 D2C diverges sharply from the
  simulation (predicting strongly negative, unphysical Poisson ratios with
  large variance), while the area-fixed version stays much closer to the
  simulation ground truth.

- At eta > 0.45: both D2C variants struggle due to nearly-degenerate
  triangles in the crystal topology, but the area-fixed version still
  outperforms the old one.

The plot (area_fix_comparison.png) shows these results clearly.


The Problem
-----------
Despite the empirical improvement, the 1/Area normalization does not arise
from the D2C derivation.  The continuum limit that produces the elastic
tensor does not introduce a per-triangle area density factor in this way.
The factor of 1/16 in the original code is a direct consequence of the
algebra of the derivation (expanding bond vectors into strain contributions),
not an approximation that assumed equilateral triangles.

In other words: the area fix "works better" numerically but lacks
theoretical justification.  It is an ad-hoc correction that happens to
compensate for other sources of error (likely the mean-field/self-consistent
approximation breaking down when triangles become heterogeneous), rather
than fixing a genuine bug in the derivation.


Recommendation
--------------
DO NOT adopt the 1/Area fix.  The improvement is empirical and coincidental,
not grounded in the theory.  Incorporating it would mean the code no longer
implements the actual D2C derivation, making future theoretical work
(error bounds, higher-order corrections, etc.) unreliable.

The correct path forward is to understand WHY the original derivation loses
accuracy at large eta — which is a question about the mean-field
approximation and the self-consistent coupling, not about area normalization.

# S1 — the v2 head: parametrisation verified, first training runs

**What.** Stage S1 of `Phase 5/m2/M2_V2_PLAN.md`: build the section 2.1 head, gate it, and find out
whether it learns. Two questions, answered separately because they have different answers.

**Producers.** `Phase 5/m2/model_v2.py` (head) · `Phase 5/verifications/test_m2_head.py` (gates) ·
`Phase 5/m2/train_v2.py` (training) · dataset `Phase 5/m2/build_dataset.py` (gitignored, rebuild in
~16 min). Raw output: `train_runs_2026-08-26.log`; per-run metrics `run_within.json`,
`run_bravais.json`. Commit `b8bd8a9`, threads pinned to 1 (D4), float64.

---

## 1. The parametrisation — VERIFIED, and before any training

These test the head's *form*, not its weights, so they settle in seconds. If the head could not
represent the answer with oracle weights, no amount of training would help.

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| [1] closed form on the OPEN single triangle | **2.78e-17** over 3 shapes × 3 `k` vectors |
| [2] closed form on an affine cell (`W`≡0) | 1.39e-17 |
| **[3] EXPRESSIVE with `W` ≠ 0** | **3.52e-16** at `max|W|` = 2.43 |
| [3b] off-diagonals carry the non-affine content | ratio 0.533 (0 would mean affine-only) |
| [4] equivariance `C → 𝓡C𝓡ᵀ` | 5.22e-16 at θ = 0.70 |
| [5] SPD by construction | min eig −2.6e-12 over 500 adversarial raw weights |
| [6] intensivity of mean pooling | 1.97e-16 across n_tri = 32, 96, 128 |

**[3] is the decisive one.** Solving `Q G Qᵀ = C(s)` per triangle recovers the solver exactly even
under strong non-affine response. The plan's *earlier* ansatz — a scalar-weighted sum of the bare
`q_e q_eᵀ` — is affine-only and would have failed it. That error was caught in review; this gate is
what keeps it caught.

**Units, measured not assumed.** The closed form is `c_e = k_e/(16 ℓ_e²)` in the solver's INTERNAL
units. The plan states the gate as `k/(4ℓ²)`, which is the PHYSICAL form — a factor 4 apart. Left
unnoticed, S1 would have "failed" on a convention.

## 2. Training — it learns, but does NOT yet beat the trivial local baseline

150 epochs, batch 32, hidden 64 × 4 layers, 117 702 parameters. Trusted training set 9 715 samples
(176 untrusted excluded, 84 held-out large), 104 156 effective local samples.

| | per-triangle MAE/std | bulk MAE/std | SPD violations |
|---|---|---|---|
| within-family (trajectory split) — **memorisation baseline, not a result** | 0.4613 | 0.2146 | **0** |
| **leave-one-family-out (`bravais`)** — the headline | **0.5343** | 0.3574 | **0** |

**Generalisation gap: +16 % per-triangle, +66 % bulk.** That gap is the number `M2.md` records the
July validation as never having had, because it split randomly and scored against its own training
labels.

### The baselines it has to beat — and on held-out family it does not

| predictor | MAE/std |
|---|---|
| the global mean everywhere | 0.6965 |
| **each sample's OWN bulk value at every triangle** | **0.5144** |
| model, within-family | 0.4613 |
| model, held-out family | **0.5343** ← *worse than the bulk baseline* |

60.2 % of the label variance is *within*-sample, so there is real local structure available. Within
family the model beats "predict your own bulk" by ~10 %. **On an unseen family it does not beat it
at all** — it has essentially learned the bulk response and not the local mechanics, which is the
thing per-triangle supervision was adopted to teach.

**SPD violation rate is 0 in both runs.** This is not an achievement of training: `C = Q·MMᵀ·Qᵀ` is
positive semi-definite as algebra. Reported because a nonzero value would mean a bug.

## 3. It is UNDERFIT, not overfit

Train loss was still falling at epoch 149 (0.378, from 59.2) and validation was still improving.
The train/val gap is negligible. So the ceiling here is **not** data volume and **not**
over-parameterisation (1.13 params per effective sample) — it is training length, or the feature
set, or the architecture. Longer training is the cheap thing to eliminate first.

## 4. Limitations, and mistakes made getting here

- **Not comparable to the plan's success criteria yet.** §1's must-tier is `MAE(ν) ≤ 0.02` and
  `MAE(E)/E ≤ 5 %` **against the independent sim**. This reports normalised `C6` error against
  solver labels. The ν/E comparison is not done, so **S1 cannot be called passed or failed** on the
  plan's terms.
- **One family held out, not five.** §4 specifies 5-fold leave-one-family-out; `bravais` alone is a
  single fold and the easiest kind of family (ordered crystals).
- **The held-out large-size bin was never scored.** It exists (84 samples at ~1000 triangles) and is
  excluded from training, but the size-generalisation number — the real test of the locality result
  in `M2_LOCALITY.md` — has not been measured.
- **A 10× runtime misestimate.** I predicted 12 min/run and it took 2 h, because I benchmarked
  `raw[:512]`, and the builder yields `cells` first — so I timed 512 of the *smallest* graphs and
  extrapolated to a mix containing 240- and 1000-triangle ones. Benchmark a random draw, not the head
  of an ordered list.
- **The first two runs nearly lost their own results.** Output went to a session scratchpad, and the
  checkpoint name was fixed, so run B silently overwrote run A's weights. Fixed: checkpoints now
  carry the split (`checkpoint_v2_<tag>.pt`) and every run writes `run_<tag>.json` into this
  directory, including the learning curve, so convergence can be judged without re-running.

## 5. What follows

1. Train materially longer — it had not converged, and that is the cheapest explanation to rule out.
2. Report `ν, E` against the **independent sim**, so the numbers meet §1's criteria.
3. Score the held-out large bin.
4. Only then judge the features: if a converged model still fails to beat the bulk baseline on a
   held-out family, the invariant feature set (`k/k̄`, `log k/k̄`, `ℓ`, degree) is too thin to
   express a local environment — and *that* is the real S1 finding.

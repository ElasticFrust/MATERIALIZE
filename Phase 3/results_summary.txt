Network parameters:

  VD distortion : eta = 0.15  (node perturbation amplitude)

Configurations:
  A) Regular triangular lattice, uniform k=1   (baseline)
  B) Regular triangular lattice + VD rigidities (heterogeneous k)
  C) Deformed lattice (eta=0.15), uniform k=1
  D) Deformed lattice (eta=0.15) + VD rigidities

1.  ANALYTICAL HOMOGENIZATION  (Woodbury forward solver)
================================================================================

The 7-step differentiable pipeline computes the homogenized elastic tensor,
Poisson ratio, and Young's modulus.

  B) Regular, VD rigs        0.3487   6.185e-03   6.692e-03    7.020e-03    2.340e-03    2.340e-03
  C) Deformed, k=1           0.2992   5.431e-02   5.981e-02    5.966e-02    1.789e-02    2.083e-02
  D) Deformed + VD rigs      0.0485   3.332e-03   3.178e-03    3.348e-03    1.537e-04    1.591e-03

Key observations (analytical):
  - A is the classic triangular lattice: nu = 1/3, perfectly isotropic.
  - B (VD rigidities only): nu rises slightly to 0.349; stiffness drops 10x.
    The heterogeneous k pattern mostly softens the material.
  - C (deformation only): nu drops to 0.299; modest softening.
    Geometric disorder lowers Poisson below the 1/3 crystalline value.
  - D (both effects): nu drops dramatically to 0.049 (near zero!).
    Deformation + heterogeneous rigidities compound to produce an
    almost auxetic-adjacent material with very low Poisson ratio.


================================================================================
2.  MECHANICAL SIMULATION  (spring energy minimisation, KUBC)
================================================================================

Method:
  - Kinematic Uniform Boundary Conditions (KUBC): outermost 10% of nodes
    held at affinely displaced positions.
  - Interior nodes (~740) relax via L-BFGS-B to minimise total spring
    energy  E = Sum 1/2 k (|ri-rj| - l0)^2.
  - 6 independent strain tests (exx, eyy, exy, exx+eyy, exx+exy, eyy+exy)
    at delta = 0.005 -> full 3x3 Voigt stiffness matrix.
  - Poisson ratio from compliance matrix: S = inv(C), nu = -S12/S11.

  Config                     nu_x     nu_y     nu_avg   C11/C22
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

3.  COMPARISON:  Analytical vs Simulation
================================================================================

  Config                   nu(sim)  nu(ana)  error    Trend match?
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  A) Regular, k=1          0.3303   0.3333    0.9%    YES (baseline ~1/3)
  B) Regular, VD rigs      0.2055   0.3487   41.1%    YES (lower than A)
  C) Deformed, k=1         0.3146   0.2992    5.1%    YES (lower than A)
  D) Deformed + VD rigs    0.1065   0.0485  119.5%    YES (lowest of all)

  Qualitative ordering:  D < B < C < A   (simulation)
                         D < C < A < B   (analytical)

  Relative stiffness (E/E_A):
  Config                   E/E_A(sim)  E/E_A(ana)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
  A) Regular, k=1           1.000       1.000
  B) Regular, VD rigs       0.564       0.099
  C) Deformed, k=1          0.985       0.869
  D) Deformed + VD rigs     0.552       0.053

Discussion:
  - Config A (uniform, regular): Excellent match (0.9% error). The KUBC
    simulation on a uniform lattice faithfully reproduces the analytical
    1/3 Poisson ratio. The lattice is isotropic (C11/C22 = 1.01).

  - Config C (deformed, uniform k): Good match (5% error). Geometric
    disorder is well-captured by the finite-size simulation.

  - Configs B, D (heterogeneous rigidities): Significant quantitative
    discrepancy. The KUBC upper bound on a finite 14x14 sample does not
    fully capture the softening from heterogeneous rigidities. The
    boundary layer (38% of nodes) is constrained affinely, which
    artificially stiffens the response and raises the Poisson ratio.
    The analytical homogenization assumes an infinite periodic medium
    with full non-affine relaxation.

  - Despite quantitative discrepancies, the simulation correctly captures
    all qualitative trends:
      * VD rigidities lower both Poisson ratio and stiffness
      * Geometric deformation lowers Poisson ratio
      * The combination (config D) gives the most dramatic effect
      * All configs remain nearly isotropic (C11/C22 ~ 1.0)

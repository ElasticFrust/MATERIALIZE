## 2. Open findings

### A — verification logic

**A-1 · correctness risk · `designer.verify()` measures against the wrong oracle.**
[`Phase 5/designer.py:82-97`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L82-L97) computes `solver_sim_gap` against
`sim_per_triangle_C6`, which routes the simulation's relaxation through the solver's own
contraction. `CLAUDE.md` §3 names the physical ground truth as virial/energy (`physical_homog`).
Post-fix the two nearly agree, but they are not identical and the check is structurally
self-referential: on the three designs that fail it, gap-vs-sim and gap-vs-physical differ
(`seed_honeycomb` 0.3135 vs 0.3666; `seed_square_octagon` 0.5127 vs 0.5738). *Fix: use
`physical_homog.energy_C` as the oracle in `verify()`.*

**A-2 · correctness risk · `target_err_sim` mixes ν and E in one absolute max — and it is the
ranking key.** [`designer.py:102-103`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L102-L103) builds
`max(|Δν|, |ΔE|)`; [`designer.py:217`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L217) sorts kept designs by it. For
targets with E ≫ 1 the E term dominates and ν is effectively ignored in ranking; the solver-vs-sim
gap beside it is *relative*, so the two headline numbers use different scales. *Fix: make it
relative and weight the channels, as the gap does.*

**A-3 · correctness risk · design gap is reported against the sim only.** `CLAUDE.md` §3 (B) requires
achieved−target on **both** the solver and the independent sim. `verify()` returns only
`target_err_sim`; the solver-side residual is available (the optimiser's own loss) but never
recorded alongside it.

**A-4 · correctness risk · no `UnhealthyGeometryError` handling in the designer.**
`verify()`/`design()` ([`designer.py:74`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L74),
[`:209`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L209)) call the sim inside a loop over candidates with no
`try/except`. The sim self-protects by *raising* (by design), so a single sliver candidate aborts an
entire search. `CLAUDE.md` §3: "callers just `try/except` it".

**A-5 · correctness risk · the caller-side physicality gate is missing.** `CLAUDE.md` §3 assigns the
"is the RESPONSE physical" check (solver ν,E finite) to the caller/designer, since it needs the
solver and the sim must not depend on it. `phys_targets.is_physical`
([`:132-149`](../Phase%205/phys_targets.py#L132-L149)) is a solid filter — SPD, finite, E in range,
|ν| bounded, reciprocity — but it is applied **only to generated TARGETS**, inside `phys_targets.py`
itself (`:207`, `:251`, `:289`). No designer path applies it, or anything like it, to a designed
network's achieved response. So the gate CLAUDE.md specifies does not exist; a near-mechanism design
is caught only if the solver-vs-sim gap happens to notice it.

**A-6 · hygiene · hardcoded tolerance shadows the parameter.**
[`designer.py:324`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L324) asserts a literal `0.05` instead of the
`gap_tol` argument it was given.

**A-7 · blocker (for the affected code paths) · `Ceff_nuE` was tombstoned without updating its
callers.** [`test_cluster_Ceff.py:65-77`](../verification_tools/test_cluster_Ceff.py#L65-L77) now
raises `NotImplementedError`, but it is still called live from **five** modules, each of which would
crash on first use: `test_cluster_Ceff.py:142-144`, `test_cluster_Ceff_rigidity.py:70,73,99`,
`test_cluster_VD.py:85,86,106`, `test_intrinsic_metric.py:127-129`,
`verify_gb_formulation.py:96,98`.

The removal itself was right — `Ceff_nuE` was the legacy **area-weighted** mean of C(s), which is
physically wrong (`A(s)` carries no area prefactor, so the energy is an unweighted *sum* and its
Δg-Hessian is the unweighted *mean*; area-weighting biases ν on unequal-area meshes and overstates
auxeticity). What was left undone is the callers.

**Consequence for the evidence base.** Those call sites are the legacy comparison studies
themselves. [`test_intrinsic_metric.py:127-129`](../verification_tools/test_intrinsic_metric.py#L127-L129)

"Configuration-free metric solve reproduces the simulation Hessian". That is the study behind
`CLAUDE.md` §3's full-constraints rule and its "classic single-site mean-field ≈1.4× over-compliance
error". **The conclusion is safe but its stated evidence is stale:** intrinsic-over-MF is now
confirmed far more strongly and independently (test [15] vs the energy-Hessian tensor, 1.6e-12; the
paired probes, 2.7e-8). The specific ≈1.4× figure traces to numbers computed with the superseded
average and has not been reproduced. *Do not re-cite it without re-deriving it.*

**A-15 · blocker (verification logic) · the oracle is LINEAR, so "checked against the independent
simulation" cannot mean what CLAUDE.md §3 claimed it means.** Found 2026-08-15 while measuring
whether the solver and the sim agree. `physical_homog.relax` is a single `spsolve` of Ku = −f_aff
using the linearised bond extension (R·δu)/ℓ — no iteration, no Newton, no minimisation — and a
repo-wide search finds **no nonlinear relaxation anywhere**. §3 described the verification path as an
"independent NumPy periodic *nonlinear* relaxation"; that word was false and is now corrected.

Two consequences pull in opposite directions and both matter:

  - *Stronger than assumed where it applies.* Because both sides solve the SAME linear problem, the
    solver's own `forward()` matches the oracle to **1e-13 – 1e-11** on healthy disordered and VD
    networks (η up to 0.40), and still to ~1e-3 at VD a=+100 where E ~ 1e-7. The intrinsic
    constrained metric solve is numerically equivalent to the nodal solve. That is a genuine, sharp
    check of the implementation — it is what would catch an A-0-class contraction bug.
  - *Absent where it was most claimed.* §3 justifies checking every design against the sim precisely
    because "near a mechanism ... the linear read-back diverges from the true nonlinear relaxation".
    A linear oracle diverges identically, so it cannot detect that. **Near-mechanism designs are not
    currently validated against nonlinear physics by anything in the repo.**

Note this also reframes the O(DELTA)≈1e-3 residuals in [7]/[8]: those feed the contraction the sim's
GEOMETRICALLY EXACT W (`tri_metric_change`, Δg = FᵀF − I) and compare against the LINEARISED energy
oracle, so the floor is a strain-measure mismatch between the two, not solver error. The solver's own
end-to-end agreement is 8 orders better.

*Fix (not attempted here — needs a decision): either add a genuinely nonlinear relaxation to the
oracle (a Newton/minimisation solve on the true spring energy Σ½k(‖x_i−x_j‖−ℓ₀)²) and gate the
near-mechanism regime against it, or drop the nonlinear-validation claim from §3 and state plainly
that the framework is validated only in the linear regime. The first is the honest completion of
FUTURE_DIRECTIONS #1; the second is free but narrows what may be claimed.*

**A-10 · correctness risk · CHARACTERISED PRECISELY 2026-08-15, still OPEN (needs a convention
decision).** It is not "three formulas that drift": **one is DIRECTIONAL and one is
DIRECTION-AVERAGED**, and they are each self-consistent.

  - design path — `forward_solver_torch.py:460-467` (the PROTECTED core), `inverse_design.c6_to_nuE`,
    `_common.c6_nuE` — returns **ν_yx and E_y, the y-direction only**;
  - oracle — `physical_homog._voigt_nuE` — returns **½(ν_xy+ν_yx) and ½(Ex+Ey)**.

Verified to 1e-12 against directional values computed from the compliance on two tensors. They agree
**exactly on isotropic** tensors — which is why the ν=1/3 gate has never seen it — and diverge on any
anisotropic one, including a plain ORTHOTROPIC tensor with zero shear coupling: for
`C=[[2.0,0.6,0],[0.6,1.3,0],[0,0,0.5]]` the oracle gives ν=0.3808, E=1.4215 while the design path
gives ν=0.3000, E=1.1200 — **Δν = 0.081, ΔE/E = 21%**. So every anisotropic "achieved vs target"
comparison between designer and oracle mixes two different physical quantities.

Neither is *wrong*; the design path's pair is a consistent description of y-loading. But it is
undocumented (`c6_to_nuE`'s docstring says only "the SAME formula the solver's forward uses"),
silently asymmetric in x/y, and it means a scalar `nu`/`E` objective secretly targets the
y-direction while `nu_theta`/`E_theta` exist for direction-resolved targets. **Deciding the canonical
convention is a user call**: making it direction-averaged edits the protected core and retroactively
changes the recorded "achieved ν" of every saved anisotropic design. A-9's gates deliberately compare
TENSORS component-wise, so they are immune to this. Original finding follows.

### B — guideline adherence

**B-1 · correctness risk · WIDER THAN RECORDED (measured 2026-08-15) — it is not only `optimize()`.**
Two separate manifestations, both on an unchanged tree:

  - *Tiny, everywhere:* `forward(method='intrinsic')` is not bit-reproducible. The SAME `make_solver`
    called twice in one process gives ν differing by 1.7e-16 (two adjacent doubles), C_eff by
    2.2e-16, per-triangle C6 by 5.0e-16 — run-ORDER dependent, suggesting BLAS/threading warm-up or
    an accumulation-order effect rather than a seed.
  - *Eight orders, between processes:* `test_inverse_design` **[15]**'s solver-vs-physical tensor
    agreement on the η=0.35 disordered mesh reports **1.6e-12 on one run and 1.4e-04 on the next,
    from the identical tree** (verified by running the same commit twice; [14] and [16] shift with
    it). Within a single process it is perfectly stable — 1.60e-12 across 5 repeats, spread 1e-15 —
    and it does not move after a preceding `optimize()`, so the trigger is process state, not
    arithmetic order inside a run.

Both stay far inside the asserts, so nothing is *wrong* today. But it means **"the solver matches the
physical oracle to 1e-12" is not a claim that survives a re-run** — sometimes it is 1e-4. Any

## 3. Method and its limits

Tier 1 = deep read (solver contraction surface, `_common`, `physical_homog`, `designer`,
`inverse_design`, both regression suites). Tier 2 = mechanical detectors over ~90 remaining scripts,
deep-read on a hit. **Detector-based, not exhaustive**, and two detectors were found to over-fire and
their raw output discarded: "no float64" (missed that importing `_common` sets it process-wide) and
"no module docstring" (missed `r"""` raw-string docstrings). Where a detector's output is not
reproduced as a named finding above, treat that area as unaudited rather than clean.

## 4. Not covered by this pass

Treat these as **unaudited**, not clean:

- `Phase 5/seeds.py` and `triangulation.py` — not deep-read (the topology generators; note the
  designer's non-Delaunay claim rests on `triangulation.random_flipped_geo`).
- Phase 2 and Phase 3 guideline adherence **beyond the verification path** — the solver's numerical
  internals, the objective/constraint machinery, and the strain/stress design family were read only
  where the verification logic touched them.
- The ~90 Tier-2 scripts were covered by **detectors only**; a detector hit that is not reproduced as
  a named finding above was discarded, not cleared. Two detectors were themselves defective (float64:
  missed that importing `_common` sets it process-wide; docstring: missed `r"""`).
- Systematic in-file documentation quality (B-10) beyond the specific stale-docstring findings, and
  out-of-file entry-point coverage (B-11).
- `Phase 4/` (frozen remnant) and the root legacy tier, excluded by scope.

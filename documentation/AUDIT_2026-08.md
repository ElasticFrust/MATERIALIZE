## 2. Open findings

### A — verification logic

**A-1 · correctness risk · `designer.verify()` measures against the wrong oracle.**
[`Phase 5/designer.py:82-97`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L82-L97) computes `solver_sim_gap` against
`sim_per_triangle_C6`, which routes the simulation's relaxation through the solver's own
contraction. `CLAUDE.md` §3 names the physical ground truth as virial/energy (`physical_homog`).
Post-fix the two nearly agree, but they are not identical and the check is structurally
self-referential: on the three designs that fail it, gap-vs-sim and gap-vs-physical differ
(`seed_honeycomb` 0.3135 vs 0.3666; `seed_square_octagon` 0.5127 vs 0.5738). *Fix: use
`physical_homog.energy_C` as the oracle in `verify()`.*

**A-2 · correctness risk · `target_err_sim` mixes ν and E in one absolute max — and it is the
ranking key.** [`designer.py:102-103`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L102-L103) builds
`max(|Δν|, |ΔE|)`; [`designer.py:217`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L217) sorts kept designs by it. For
targets with E ≫ 1 the E term dominates and ν is effectively ignored in ranking; the solver-vs-sim
gap beside it is *relative*, so the two headline numbers use different scales. *Fix: make it
relative and weight the channels, as the gap does.*

**A-3 · correctness risk · design gap is reported against the sim only.** `CLAUDE.md` §3 (B) requires
achieved−target on **both** the solver and the independent sim. `verify()` returns only
`target_err_sim`; the solver-side residual is available (the optimiser's own loss) but never
recorded alongside it.

**A-4 · correctness risk · no `UnhealthyGeometryError` handling in the designer.**
`verify()`/`design()` ([`designer.py:74`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L74),
[`:209`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L209)) call the sim inside a loop over candidates with no
`try/except`. The sim self-protects by *raising* (by design), so a single sliver candidate aborts an
entire search. `CLAUDE.md` §3: "callers just `try/except` it".

**A-5 · correctness risk · the caller-side physicality gate is missing.** `CLAUDE.md` §3 assigns the
"is the RESPONSE physical" check (solver ν,E finite) to the caller/designer, since it needs the
solver and the sim must not depend on it. `phys_targets.is_physical`
([`:132-149`](../Phase%205/phys_targets.py#L132-L149)) is a solid filter — SPD, finite, E in range,
|ν| bounded, reciprocity — but it is applied **only to generated TARGETS**, inside `phys_targets.py`
itself (`:207`, `:251`, `:289`). No designer path applies it, or anything like it, to a designed
network's achieved response. So the gate CLAUDE.md specifies does not exist; a near-mechanism design
is caught only if the solver-vs-sim gap happens to notice it.

**A-6 · hygiene · hardcoded tolerance shadows the parameter.**
[`designer.py:324`](../Phase%205/designer.py#L324) asserts a literal `0.05` instead of the
`gap_tol` argument it was given.

**A-7 · blocker (for the affected code paths) · `Ceff_nuE` was tombstoned without updating its
callers.** [`test_cluster_Ceff.py:65-77`](../verification_tools/test_cluster_Ceff.py#L65-L77) now
raises `NotImplementedError`, but it is still called live from **five** modules, each of which would
crash on first use: `test_cluster_Ceff.py:142-144`, `test_cluster_Ceff_rigidity.py:70,73,99`,
`test_cluster_VD.py:85,86,106`, `test_intrinsic_metric.py:127-129`,
`verify_gb_formulation.py:96,98`.

The removal itself was right — `Ceff_nuE` was the legacy **area-weighted** mean of C(s), which is
physically wrong (`A(s)` carries no area prefactor, so the energy is an unweighted *sum* and its
Δg-Hessian is the unweighted *mean*; area-weighting biases ν on unequal-area meshes and overstates
auxeticity). What was left undone is the callers.

**Consequence for the evidence base.** Those call sites are the legacy comparison studies
themselves. [`test_intrinsic_metric.py:127-129`](../verification_tools/test_intrinsic_metric.py#L127-L129)
computes *all three* curves — sim, intrinsic, single-site MF — through `Ceff_nuE`, under the title
"Configuration-free metric solve reproduces the simulation Hessian". That is the study behind
`CLAUDE.md` §3's full-constraints rule and its "classic single-site mean-field ≈1.4× over-compliance
error". **The conclusion is safe but its stated evidence is stale:** intrinsic-over-MF is now
confirmed far more strongly and independently (test [15] vs the energy-Hessian tensor, 1.6e-12; the
paired probes, 2.7e-8). The specific ≈1.4× figure traces to numbers computed with the superseded
average and has not been reproduced. *Do not re-cite it without re-deriving it.*

**Retirement scope (approved 2026-08-14; execution deferred).** The obvious move — delete the five
— is **wrong**: four of them are load-bearing. Verified by import graph:

| module | verdict |
|---|---|
| `test_cluster_Ceff.py` | **KEEP** — `vec3`, `tri_metric_change`, `bare_tensor`, `c6_to_nuE`, `DELTA`, `MODES`; imported by 20+ files incl. `_common.py` and `test_forward_solver.py` [7] |
| `test_cluster_VD.py` | **KEEP** — `build_geometry`/`set_VD`; imported by **`inverse_design.py:34`**, on which `DesignProblem.periodic` depends |
| `test_cluster_rigidity.py` | **KEEP** — `assemble_K_faff`, `bare_tensor`; imported nearly everywhere |
| `test_cluster_Ceff_rigidity.py` | **KEEP module** — `mf_W3` imported by `test_cluster_VD.py:16`, `test_intrinsic_VD.py:22` |
| `test_intrinsic_metric.py` | **KEEP module** — `edge_op`/`curv_op`/`mean_op`/`intrinsic_solve` imported by `test_intrinsic_VD.py:24`, `verify_gb_formulation.py:26` |
| `verify_gb_formulation.py` | **DELETE** — imported by nothing; standalone legacy study whose numbers came from `Ceff_nuE` |

So: delete one file; inside the others retire only the dead comparison paths (`Ceff_nuE` itself, the
`main()`/`nuE_all` routines that call it, and the `mf_*` mean-field helpers if nothing else uses
them). Deferred to a cleanup pass after the audit.

**A-7b · correctness risk (architecture) · the shared geometry/assembly library lives in files named
`test_*` inside the oracle layer.** `build_geometry`, `assemble_K_faff`, `bare_tensor`, `vec3`,
`tri_metric_change` are the foundation of Phases 2, 3 and 5, but they sit in
`verification_tools/test_cluster_*.py`. `Phase 3/inverse_design.py` — the design layer — therefore
imports "tests" from the temporary-oracle layer, inverting the intended dependency direction
(`CLAUDE.md` §2: dependencies point inward; the oracle is retireable). It also makes dead code look
deletable when it is not, which is exactly the trap A-7 sprang. *Fix: factor the live helpers into a
properly named shared module the core layers can own, leaving the `test_*` files as studies.*

**A-8 · open todo · `_common.open_stretch` is not guarded by `require_healthy_mesh`**, unlike the PBC
sim, and the open-boundary verification suite remains incomplete (pre-existing todo 2.6).

**A-9 · deferred (agreed) · per-triangle `C(s)` is not gated by any independent oracle.** Only bulk
`C_eff` is checked; local/regional objectives and the per-triangle field maps consume `C(s)`
directly. Scheduled after this audit.

### B — guideline adherence

**B-1 · correctness risk · the design path is not reproducible.** Repeated identical `optimize()`
calls — same seed, same code, single-threaded — give different results, and at `reg=0` the outcome
is **bistable**: on `test_local_region`'s problem, err 0.0000 or 0.0664 (`k_min/mean ~1e-8`, a
near-mechanism), flipping pass/fail. This breaks "every artifact traceable to (commit, config,
seed)". Source not identified.

**B-2 · correctness risk · `reg` still omitted at 11 `optimize()` call sites in the gate itself**
(`test_inverse_design.py` lines 29, 51, 78, 90, 102, 114, 118, 123, 240, 251, 281, 292, 372, 379;
[2]/[4]/[9] were fixed). `CLAUDE.md` §3: "you MUST pass reg". They pass today but carry the same
latent bistability as B-1. **Confirmed scope: the test file only** — every other detector hit
(`positions.py:46`, `mode_selective*.py`, `convergence_check.py:4`, `find_profile.py:6`,
`three_ribbon.py:104`, `fig1_recreate_pointy.py:6`) is the word "optimize()" inside a comment or
docstring; the Phase 3 verification scripts all pass `reg=REG`.
**Exemption, established experimentally:** `test_round_trip` legitimately must *not* use reg — it
recovers a known `k_true`, and a k-variance penalty biases k away from it (forcing `reg=0.01`
repo-wide makes it the only failure of 15, err 2.09e-03 vs a 1e-3 tolerance). Worth writing into
§3 as a stated exception rather than leaving it as an accident.

**B-3 · hygiene · `save_network` records no seed and no commit.**
[`_common.py:703-714`](../Phase%203/verifications/_common.py#L703-L714) stores target, gap and
`is_fictional` but neither the seed nor the code version, so no saved design satisfies the charter's
traceability requirement. (`Phase 5/` being untracked until ccb2fbe made this moot; it no longer is.)

**B-3b · correctness risk · `positions.py`'s own API cheat-sheet omits the parameter whose omission
is the documented failure mode.** The module header's "Public API" block
([`positions.py:30-35`](../Phase%205/positions.py#L30-L35)) lists
`loss_at(geo, k, nu_target, E_target)`, `spsa_positions(..., redelaunay_every=0)` and
`design_with_positions(..., reg=0.02, redelaunay_every=0)` — none showing `nu_weight`/`E_weight`,
which all three actually take ([`:96`](../Phase%205/positions.py#L96),
[`:111-112`](../Phase%205/positions.py#L111-L112),
[`:205-208`](../Phase%205/positions.py#L205-L208)). `CLAUDE.md` §3 makes weight-matching the
critical rule of position optimisation ("else it silently optimises a different loss and destroys
anisotropy"), and the in-body docstring at `:100` says "MUST match" — so a reader following the
module's own cheat-sheet is led into exactly the documented failure. *Fix: sync the header block.*

**B-4 · hygiene · ~27 one-off `plot_*`/`draw_*` remain** outside `plotting.py`, including
`_common.draw_network` / `draw_lattice_zoom`, which `CLAUDE.md` §3 marks as replaced by the
canonical tiled-continuous draw. Migration is opportunistic by policy; listed for completeness.

## 3. Method and its limits

Tier 1 = deep read (solver contraction surface, `_common`, `physical_homog`, `designer`,
`inverse_design`, both regression suites). Tier 2 = mechanical detectors over ~90 remaining scripts,
deep-read on a hit. **Detector-based, not exhaustive**, and two detectors were found to over-fire and
their raw output discarded: "no float64" (missed that importing `_common` sets it process-wide) and
"no module docstring" (missed `r"""` raw-string docstrings). Where a detector's output is not
reproduced as a named finding above, treat that area as unaudited rather than clean.
